# Art direction

Interpret "Pulse-like" as a compact, evocative cover tied to the user's current material.
Do not claim an exact historical Pulse template. When a user provides an example, inspect it
and extract observable traits: subject scale, negative space, material, palette, light, and
emotional tone. The source idea should survive beyond the prompt and into the visible image.

## Keep identity; vary expression

Start with one identifying shape or relationship from the selected element. Write the visual
connection in a sentence, then choose a medium that strengthens it. Prefer one clear focal
idea over a collage of every term in the source. Make references functional: a subject image
constrains structure, a material reference changes surface, and a prior favorite informs taste.

For scientific material, distinguish a literal stylized illustration from an abstract metaphor
in the brief and caption. A branching vascular tree may become translucent glass filaments;
its connected branching remains. A hypothesis about cellular communication may become a
scene of separated lights; the caption must identify this as an artistic interpretation.

## Starting directions

These are original prompt ingredients, not fixed templates or claims about trained models.
Use the user's library first. Adapt, combine, or replace these when the subject calls for it.

| Direction | Observable treatment | Useful conceptual fit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Layered paper | Cut edges, shallow relief, restrained color, directional shadows | Compartments, layers, nested systems |
| Botanical ink wash | Fine branching strokes, soft pigment blooms, generous empty space | Growth, branching, delicate networks |
| Translucent glass | Luminous translucent material, restrained refraction, dark or neutral field | Connected pathways and boundaries |
| Embroidered field | Visible threads, stitched contours, woven ground | Connectivity, repeated units, repair |
| Ceramic relief | Matte clay, low relief, raking light, earthy glaze | Topology, organization, tangible structure |
| Risograph print | Limited inks, halftone texture, slight registration offsets | Strong silhouettes, repeated patterns |
| Quiet surreal landscape | One enlarged subject, spare horizon, improbable but coherent scale | Discovery, uncertainty, relationships |
| Luminous pigment | Soft color fields, sparse bright accents, subtle grain | Gradients, transitions, distribution |

Do not pretend that a named medium guarantees that the backend will reproduce it. Check the
actual result. Change more than just the palette when exploring a new direction. Conversely,
when the user favorites a style, explore compositions within it instead of forcing rotation.

## Prompt shape

State the subject and its identifying relationships before the artistic treatment. Describe
composition and material in concrete visual language. Include what each attached reference
controls and what must not be copied. Finish with framing, aspect ratio, text preference, and
subject-specific exclusions. Save this full prompt locally before calling the generator.

Example using a synthetic subject:

> Create a landscape 16:9 editorial artwork of a connected branching network, interpreted as
> a translucent glass sculpture. Preserve the continuous trunk and progressively finer
> branches; leave ample quiet space around the silhouette. Use cool teal glass with a few
> amber reflections, soft side lighting, a warm charcoal background, and restrained depth of
> field. The image should express how a single structure distributes flow into many paths.
> No text, labels, arrows, unrelated organs, or numerical data. This is a conceptual artwork.

Use an inspected real reference when available; this text example is not a substitute for
reading the configured personal library.
